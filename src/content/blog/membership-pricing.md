Most studios set membership prices the same way they set class packs: look at the studio across town, knock a little off, and hope it covers rent. It usually does, until the kiln room fills up and the wheels are booked solid every evening by the same six people. Here is how we would price memberships if we were starting again.

## Why a studio is not a gym

A gym member who comes in every day costs the gym almost nothing extra. The treadmill is already there. A coworking member who stays late uses a little more electricity. In both cases, time is the thing being sold, and extra time is nearly free.

A pottery studio sells time too, but time is not where the cost is. Clay moves through stages: thrown, trimmed, dried on a shelf, bisque fired, glazed, glaze fired, waiting for pickup. Every one of those stages takes shelf space or kiln space, and the kiln is where the real money goes. A member who spends four hours trimming three bowls costs you far less than a member who throws thirty mugs in two hours.

That is why firing cost is measured in cubic inches, not hours. Price a membership on wheel time alone and your most productive members are being subsidised by everyone else.

## Build tiers around what members actually use

In [our post on membership tiers](/blog/membership-tiers) we suggested Basic, Regular and Unlimited. The structure holds up. What matters for pricing is that each tier changes more than one thing at a time, so the step up feels worth it and your costs rise in line with the price.

| | Basic | Regular | Unlimited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly price | $120 | $175 | $260 |
| Wheel hours per week | 8 | 16 | No cap |
| Book ahead | 3 days | 5 days | 7 days |
| Shelves | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Firing | Pay per piece | 400 cu in included | 400 cu in included, kiln priority |
| Classes | Full price | Full price | 10% off |

A few things to notice:

- **Shelves are a cost.** Every shelf is floor space you are paying rent on. Do not give them away freely at the bottom tier, because a full shelf means a member producing more work than their tier was priced for.
- **Basic pays per piece.** The person coming in twice a week after work fires unevenly: nothing for a month, then a whole batch before the holidays. Per-piece fees match that.
- **Unlimited is mostly about access, not volume.** Priority on the kiln queue and the longest booking window are what committed members want. Keep the firing allowance the same as Regular and let per-piece fees handle anyone who goes over.

## What a firing allowance really costs

The 400 cubic inch allowance sounds generous, so it is worth working out what it costs you. Here is a single bisque and glaze cycle in a 7 cubic foot electric kiln, roughly as it ran at our old studio:

| Cost | Per bisque + glaze cycle |
| --- | --- |
| Electricity, bisque firing | $14.00 |
| Electricity, glaze firing | $20.00 |
| Element and thermocouple wear | $9.00 |
| Shelves, posts and kiln wash | $4.00 |
| Loading and unloading (3 hours of staff time) | $60.00 |
| **Total** | **$107.00** |

A 7 cubic foot kiln holds about 12,000 cubic inches, but you never pack it perfectly. Measured the way members log pieces (height × width × depth), we billed about 5,000 cubic inches per cycle. That puts your real cost at around **2.1 cents per cubic inch**.

So a member who uses the full 400 cubic inches, about five mugs, costs you roughly **$8.60 a month** in firing. If your per-piece rate is 4.5 cents per cubic inch, that same allowance would have earned you **$18** if you had charged for it.

Whether to bundle comes down to three questions:

1. **Does it make the next tier worth buying?** If members move from Basic to Regular because firing is included, the $8.60 is well spent.
2. **Do members use it?** If most Regular members fire well under 400 cubic inches, the allowance costs you less than the table suggests. If most of them blow through it every month, raise the price or lower the allowance.
3. **Is your staff time the real limit?** Loading labour is more than half the cost above. If your kiln is already running every day, bundling more firing will not fill it more cheaply. It just fills it faster.

Do not let the allowance roll over. Unused cubic inches that carry forward turn into one enormous load in December.

## Casual members, committed members and the evening problem

The quickest way to lose Basic members is to sell too many Unlimited memberships. Unlimited members are your best customers, but if there are enough of them, every weeknight wheel is gone before anyone else can book.

Booking-ahead windows are the main tool here. When Unlimited members can book 7 days ahead and Basic members 3, the committed members get first pick, which is fair, and they pay for it. But it only works if there is something left on day three. Watch your evening bookings for a month. If Basic members keep finding nothing after 5pm, you have three options:

- **Cap the Unlimited tier.** Decide how many your wheels can carry and stop selling once you hit it. A short waitlist for Unlimited does no harm.
- **Count peak hours double.** If evenings and weekends use two hours of a weekly allowance, members who can come in at 11am have a reason to.
- **Shorten the Unlimited window.** Going from 7 days to 5 still keeps Unlimited ahead of Basic, but gives everyone else a better chance.

Raising the Unlimited price is also an option, but try the booking rules first. Price changes upset people. Clear rules usually do not.

## When to offer a discounted membership

Nonprofit ceramics programs, school groups and youth access memberships are worth doing. They bring new people to clay and they are often the reason a studio feels like a community rather than a business. They should not cost you your best hours or your kiln.

Attach conditions that keep the discount from eating into paid membership:

- **Off-peak hours only.** Weekday mornings and early afternoons are when your wheels sit empty anyway. A discounted membership that books 9am to 3pm costs you very little.
- **Standard kiln priority, not more.** Discounted members go in the normal queue. They do not jump ahead of Unlimited members, and they do not get their own load unless they fill one.
- **Per-piece firing, or a smaller allowance.** Keep the wheel discount generous and the firing discount modest. Firing is where your cost is.
- **A fixed number of places.** Decide how many discounted members you can carry, put it in writing, and review it once a year.
- **A clear renewal check.** School and nonprofit rates should be renewed each term or each year, not left running forever.

In our experience, a 30 to 50% discount on these terms holds up. A 50% discount with evening access and free firing is a donation, which is fine if you mean it, but budget for it as one.

## Running it day to day

Whichever structure you choose, it only works if members can see their own rules. In Kilnbook, each tier gets its own wheel-hour limit and booking window, and members see their remaining hours on the member home screen. Firing fees are worked out by cubic inch as pieces are logged, so the kiln queue and the bill always match. When a class fills, the waitlist offers the next open seat by text without anyone at the front desk stepping in.
