A pyrometric cone is a small pyramid made of ceramic material that softens and bends when it has taken in a certain amount of heat. Each cone is numbered, and the number tells you the firing it is made for.

Cones measure **heat work**, not just temperature: a combination of how hot the kiln gets and how long it stays there. A slow firing can bend a cone at a slightly lower peak temperature than a fast one. That is why potters say "cone 6" rather than a number of degrees.

## Reading cone numbers

The scale runs in two directions:

- Numbers with a leading zero, like 06 and 04, are cooler. The more zeros and the bigger the number after them, the cooler the cone: cone 06 is cooler than cone 04.
- Numbers without a zero, like 6 and 10, are hotter. The bigger the number, the hotter: cone 10 is hotter than cone 6.

In a typical studio, bisque firings sit around cone 06 to 04, mid-range glaze firings at [cone 6](/glossary/cone-6), and high-fire glaze firings at [cone 10](/glossary/cone-10).

## Why it matters at the front desk

Every clay body and glaze is made for a cone range. A cone 6 clay in a cone 10 firing can slump or melt onto the shelf. Asking members which cone their clay is for, and keeping each cone in its own queue, prevents most firing accidents.
