A firing schedule is the set of steps a kiln runs through from start to finish. On an electric kiln with a digital controller it is usually written as segments, each with:

- **A rate**: how fast to heat or cool, in degrees per hour
- **A target**: the temperature to reach
- **A hold**: how long to stay there before moving on

A bisque schedule typically starts slowly, sometimes with a [candling](/glossary/candling) hold, to let water and organic material leave the clay. A glaze schedule can heat faster early on, then slows down near the top so the glaze melts evenly. Some glazes also need a controlled cool to develop their colour or surface.

Many controllers come with preset programs, such as slow or fast bisque and glaze firings by cone, that a studio can use as-is or adjust.

## "Firing schedule" has a second meaning

In a shared studio, people also say "firing schedule" for the calendar: which kiln fires which kind of load on which day. Both matter. The kiln program decides how the work turns out; the calendar decides when members get it back.

## Keeping a record

A firing log that notes the schedule used, the cone, the date and who loaded each kiln makes it far easier to work out what went wrong when a load comes out differently. Kilnbook keeps that log for every kiln. [How firing queues work](/features/firings).
