Cone 6 is the middle of the [cone](/glossary/pyrometric-cone) scale and the standard glaze firing in many shared studios. Electric kilns reach it comfortably, clay bodies and glazes for it are easy to buy, and it gives durable, functional pots.

## Cone 6 in a shared studio

- Most studio clay bodies sold as "mid-range" are made for cone 5 to 6.
- Because so many members work at cone 6, its glaze queue usually fills fastest and fires most often.
- A piece made from a cone 6 clay should never go into a [cone 10](/glossary/cone-10) load. It can bloat, slump or stick to the shelf.

## Why it matters at the front desk

If your studio fires both cone 6 and cone 10, the question "which clay is this?" needs an answer before the piece reaches a kiln shelf. Labelling bags of studio clay with their cone, and having members choose a cone when they log a piece, takes the guesswork out of loading.
