Bisqueware (often just "bisque") is what comes out of a [bisque firing](/glossary/bisque-firing). It is no longer clay that can be reclaimed: it is ceramic. It is still porous, which is what lets it soak up a dipped or brushed glaze and hold it in an even layer.

## Handling bisque

- Hands carry oil and lotion, which can make glaze crawl or bead. Many studios ask members to wipe or rinse bisque before glazing.
- Dust from the shelf has the same effect, so it helps to store bisque somewhere covered.
- Bisque is sturdier than [greenware](/glossary/greenware) but it still chips, so it needs its own shelf space while members wait to glaze.

## Why it matters at the front desk

The gap between "my bisque is out" and "I have glazed it" is where pieces get lost. A clear pickup shelf, a text when the bisque load is unloaded and a reminder for work left too long all keep that shelf from filling up.
