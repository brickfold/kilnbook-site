Witness cones are [pyrometric cones](/glossary/cone-numbers) placed in the kiln alongside the work. They do not control the kiln. They record what happened, so you can see afterwards whether the firing reached the cone it was meant to.

## The three-cone pack

Witness cones are often set in a pack of three, each one cone apart:

- **Guide cone**: one cone below the target. It bends first and warns you the firing is close.
- **Firing cone**: the target cone. When it bends over, the firing has reached the heatwork you wanted.
- **Guard cone**: one cone above the target. If it bends too, the load was overfired.

## Why studios use them

A kiln is rarely the same temperature everywhere. The top, middle and bottom shelves can each see a different amount of heat, and an ageing thermocouple can make a controller think it is hotter or cooler than it really is. A cone pack on several shelves shows where the hot and cool spots are, which helps when a member's glaze comes out different from last time.

Noting what the witness cones showed in the firing log, load by load, builds a record that makes those conversations much easier.
