Cone 10 sits near the top of the [cone](/glossary/pyrometric-cone) range used in studio pottery. It is the traditional temperature for stoneware and porcelain, and in many studios it is fired in a gas kiln, often in reduction for its particular glaze colours.

## Cone 10 in a shared studio

- Fewer members usually work at cone 10, so its queue fills more slowly than [cone 6](/glossary/cone-6).
- The firing is long and uses a lot of fuel, so nobody wants to fire it half empty.
- That combination is why cone 10 work can wait on a shelf for weeks, and why it causes most "when is my piece going in?" questions.

## Why it matters at the front desk

The usual fixes are a fixed firing day, a fill level that triggers the firing, or both. Posting the rule and showing members how full the load is answers most questions before they reach the desk. Read how three studios handle it in [Sharing one glaze kiln between cone 6 and cone 10 potters](/blog/glaze-firing-schedules).
