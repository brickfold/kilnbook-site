Kiln wash is a refractory coating, usually a mix of materials like alumina and kaolin in water, painted onto the top surface of kiln shelves. It does not melt at glaze temperatures. If a glaze runs off a piece during a [glaze firing](/glossary/glaze-firing), the drip lands on the wash instead of bare shelf, and it can be chipped or ground away afterwards.

Kiln wash goes on the top of the shelf only. Wash on the underside can flake off during a firing and fall onto the glazed work on the shelf below.

## Why it matters in a shared studio

In a studio where many people glaze, runny glazes and glaze on the foot are a fact of life. Shelves are expensive, and a well-washed shelf is the difference between a ten-minute cleanup and a ruined shelf.

Studios often handle this with two habits: someone on staff checks and re-washes shelves on a regular schedule, and the studio has a clear policy on who pays when a piece glues itself to a shelf. Writing that policy down, and showing it to members before they glaze, saves an awkward conversation later.
