A firing fee covers the cost of putting someone's work through the kiln: power or gas, kiln elements and shelves, kiln wash, cones and the staff time spent loading and unloading. Some studios include firing in membership dues. Others charge for it separately, especially for large pieces or for work made outside the studio.

## Common ways to charge

- **By cubic inch**: multiply height, width and depth, then charge a rate per cubic inch. It accounts for the shelf space a piece takes up, so a tall vase and a small cup are not charged the same.
- **Per piece**: a flat fee for each piece, sometimes with sizes like mug, bowl or large platter.
- **By shelf**: a member or outside customer rents part or all of a shelf in a load.

Some studios charge once for both firings, when the piece goes on the bisque shelf. Others charge for bisque and glaze separately.

## Why it gets messy

When fees are worked out by hand, the kiln log lives on a clipboard and the payments live somewhere else, and someone spends an evening matching them up.

In Kilnbook you set a rate per cubic inch, a flat fee per piece, or both. Members enter a size or pick a preset and pay when they log the piece. [How firing fees work](/features/firings).
