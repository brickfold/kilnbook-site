---
term: "Candling"
description: "Holding a kiln at a low temperature, below the boiling point of water, at the start of a firing so any moisture left in the work can escape slowly."
group: "Bisque"
---

Candling is a slow, low hold at the start of a firing, usually somewhere below the boiling point of water and often for several hours or overnight. It gives any water still in the clay time to leave gently, instead of turning to steam inside a thick wall or a closed form and cracking it.

It is most common at the start of a [bisque firing](/glossary/bisque-firing), and especially useful when:

- the load has thick pieces, sculpture or closed forms,
- work was made close to the firing date and may not be fully bone dry,
- the studio is humid and work dries slowly.

## What it means for the studio

Candling adds hours to a firing, so it changes when a load will be cool and ready to unload. If members expect their work "tomorrow", a candled bisque can push that to the day after. Writing the candle time into the kiln's firing log helps the next person who loads it.
