Candling is a slow, low-temperature hold at the start of a bisque firing. The kiln is brought up to just under the boiling point of water and held there, often for several hours or overnight, so any water still in the clay leaves as vapour rather than flashing into steam.

It is cheap insurance. A piece that felt dry on the shelf can still hold water in a thick foot or a solid handle, and candling gives that water a chance to escape before the temperature climbs.

## When studios candle

- Thick or heavy work, like sculpture, large platters and planters
- Loads where you are not sure every piece is bone dry, which in a busy shared studio is most of them
- Humid weeks, when work dries more slowly than usual

Many digital kiln controllers let you add a hold, or "preheat", segment at the start of a bisque program. If your studio candles every bisque load, write the hold into the [firing schedule](/glossary/firing-schedule) so it does not depend on who loads the kiln that day.
