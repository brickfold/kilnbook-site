Greenware is any piece that has been thrown, trimmed or built but has not been through the kiln. Potters usually describe it by how dry it is:

- **Leather hard**: firm enough to hold its shape and be trimmed, carved or have a handle attached, but still damp.
- **Bone dry**: all the water you can air-dry out of it is gone. It looks lighter in colour and feels room temperature, not cool, against your cheek.

Until it is fired, greenware is still raw clay. Drop it in a bucket of water and it slakes back down to reclaim. It is also at its most breakable: rims chip and handles snap off if a piece is picked up the wrong way.

## Why it matters in a shared studio

Damp pieces are the most common reason a bisque load goes wrong. Water left in a thick base turns to steam in the kiln and can crack the piece or blow it apart, taking a neighbour's work with it. Most studios ask members to leave work on a drying shelf until it is bone dry, and only then move it to the bisque shelf.

It also helps to have one clear rule for who may handle greenware. Members are understandably protective of unfired work, and "someone moved my bowl" is a conversation worth avoiding.
