---
term: "Greenware"
description: "Clay work that has been formed but not yet fired. It is fragile, it can still be reclaimed, and it has to be bone dry before it goes in a bisque load."
group: "Bisque"
---

Greenware is any piece that has been thrown, built or trimmed but has not been through a kiln yet. It goes through three rough stages as it dries:

- **Wet**: just off the wheel or the table, soft enough to reshape.
- **Leather-hard**: firm enough to hold its shape, still damp enough to trim, carve or attach handles.
- **Bone dry**: all the water that can leave at room temperature has gone. The piece is lighter in colour and feels room temperature, not cool, against your cheek.

Until it is fired, greenware can be slaked down and reclaimed. After the bisque firing, it cannot.

## Why it matters at the front desk

Only bone-dry greenware should go on the bisque shelf. A piece that still holds water can crack or burst in the kiln and take its neighbours with it. Most studios put a "bone dry only" sign above the shelf and still have to check.

Greenware is also the most breakable thing in the building. Shelf labels, clear names on the bottom of each piece and a fixed spot for work waiting to be fired save a lot of "who moved my mug?" conversations.
