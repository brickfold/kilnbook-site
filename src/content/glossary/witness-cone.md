A witness cone is a [pyrometric cone](/glossary/pyrometric-cone) set in the kiln as a record of the firing. After the kiln cools, how far it has bent tells you whether the load reached its target.

Potters often use three cones side by side, called a cone pack:

- **Guide cone**: one cone cooler than the target. It bends first and warns you the firing is close.
- **Firing cone**: the target cone. When its tip touches the base, the firing has reached temperature.
- **Guard cone**: one cone hotter. If it bends too, the load was over-fired.

## Why it matters at the front desk

Kiln controllers and thermocouples drift, and a load can fire hotter or cooler at the top than at the bottom. When a member says their glaze came out wrong, the witness cones from that firing are the quickest way to tell whether it was the glaze or the kiln. Noting what the cones showed in the kiln's firing log makes that answer easy to find later.
