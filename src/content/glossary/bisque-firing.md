A bisque firing (or biscuit firing) is the first trip through the kiln. It drives out the last of the water and burns off organic matter in the clay, leaving a piece that is hard enough to handle and glaze but still porous enough to soak up glaze evenly.

Most studios bisque at a low temperature, usually [cone](/glossary/pyrometric-cone) 06 or cone 04, whatever the clay body's final firing will be.

## How a bisque load is packed

Because there is no glaze to melt, bisque pieces can touch and even nest inside each other, as long as the weight is supported and nothing is jammed in tight. That is why a bisque load can hold far more work than a glaze load from the same kiln.

## What it means for the studio

- The bisque shelf is usually the busiest shelf in the building, and the first place members ask about.
- A bisque firing often starts with a slow [candling](/glossary/candling) period, so it can take longer than people expect.

In Kilnbook, each kiln has its own bisque queue. You can see how full the load is, move pieces between loads and mark it fired, and members can see where their piece is without asking. [How firing queues work](/features/firings).
