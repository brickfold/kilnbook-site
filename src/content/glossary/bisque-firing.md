A bisque firing (also called a biscuit firing, or just "bisque") is the first trip through the kiln. It burns off what is left of the water and organic material in the clay and turns it permanently into ceramic. After it, a piece can no longer be reclaimed with water.

Most studios bisque to somewhere around [cone 06 to cone 04](/glossary/cone-numbers), roughly 1,830 to 1,940°F, whatever clay the pieces are made from. That leaves the bisqueware hard enough to handle without chipping, but porous, so glaze soaks in and sticks when it is dipped, poured or brushed on.

## Loading a bisque kiln

Because there is no glaze to melt, pieces in a bisque load can touch each other. Bowls can nest, lids can stay on their pots, and small pieces can sit inside larger ones. That makes bisque loads denser than glaze loads, so a studio usually needs fewer bisque firings than glaze firings for the same amount of work.

The rule that matters is the one before loading: everything has to be [bone dry](/glossary/greenware). Many studios also [candle](/glossary/candling) the first part of the firing.

## Bisque in a shared studio

The bisque shelf is where a piece enters the studio's firing system. It is also the start of the question members ask most often: "is my bowl out yet?" Answering it starts with knowing which pieces went into which bisque load.

In Kilnbook, members log a piece from their phone when they set it on the bisque shelf. Kilnbook charges the [firing fee](/glossary/firing-fee), puts it in the right load and texts them when it is ready. [How firing queues work](/features/firings).
